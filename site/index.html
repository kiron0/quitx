Features

Everything a quit button should have been.

Built on public macOS APIs. It never reads other apps' files, and the only thing it asks the internet is whether a new QuitX release is out.

01

Quit one, some, or all

Every running app gets one row with all of its windows and helpers. Check what should go, press Quit, done. Apps still get to ask about unsaved work.

  • Search, and sort by name, CPU or memory
  • Quit, restart, or quit every other app from a row's ••• menu
  • Apps that linger in the background are asked again, so one click is enough
02

Confirm before quit

A quick “Quit 14 Apps?” before Quit All, and optionally before single quits and restarts. Return confirms, Escape cancels.

03

Normal quit or force quit

Normal quit lets apps save first. Hold ⌥ to force quit a frozen app, or make Force quit the default in Settings.

04

View background apps

Show helpers and agents that never appear in the Dock, with identical instances grouped into one row. Toggle with ⌘B.

05

Exclude apps

Add an app from its ••• menu and it disappears from the list, safe from every manual and automatic quit. Music apps can be protected with one switch.

06

Menu bar stats

Live CPU, memory, GPU and battery in the menu bar. Show any mix, as icons or text, pick how often they refresh, and click a stat for a detailed view.

07

CPU & memory warnings

When CPU or memory stays over your limit, QuitX notifies you and offers to quit inactive apps. Never the app you’re using, never without asking.

08

Quit apps when your Mac sleeps

Close everything when your Mac goes to sleep, only between the hours you pick. Wake up to a clean desktop.

09

Classic or Liquid Glass

Choose the solid classic look or translucent Liquid Glass on macOS 26. Every window and menu follows along.

10

Sounds on quit

A short arcade laser plays every time an app goes. Switch it off any time in Settings.

11

Free up busy ports

Turn on Ports in Settings and a Ports tab appears next to Apps. Every listening port shows up with its app, project folder and Docker container, refreshed live.

  • Kill one, some, or all, along with the process tree behind each port
  • Asks nicely with SIGTERM first, then SIGKILL only if the process won't let go
  • Docker containers are stopped through Docker, and system processes are never touched
  • Show the port count in the menu bar and exclude ports you always need

Last window closes, app quits

Close an app's last window and it quits with all its helpers. Switching Spaces, full screen and minimize never count.

Auto quit and timers

Quit apps you haven't touched in a while, and set a timer on one app. QuitX slows itself down in Low Power Mode.

Global shortcuts

Open the menu, Quit All or Force Quit All from anywhere with your own key combos.

Uninstall with leftovers

Remove one app or several at once, with the caches, preferences and support files they left behind. It confirms first, and everything goes to the Trash.

In-app updates

Updates itself, straight from GitHub.

No update server and no keys to trust. QuitX reads this project's GitHub Releases and checks every download before it installs.

  1. A red dot appearsWhen a new version ships, the menu bar icon gets a dot and the menu offers the update.
  2. Downloaded and verifiedThe checksum, bundle, version and code signature all have to match.
  3. Restart to UpdateQuitX swaps itself in place and reopens. Your settings stay as they were.

Turn automatic checks and background downloads on or off in Settings → General.

Install

Up and running in a minute.

QuitX is free and open source, so it isn't notarized by Apple (that needs a paid developer account). macOS asks you to confirm it once, the first time you open it. Here's exactly what you'll see.

Fastest · no prompts

Install from Terminal

Downloads the latest release from GitHub, checks its SHA-256 checksum and code signature, installs QuitX in Applications and opens it. Never run it with sudo.

curl -fsSL https://quitx.kiron.dev/install | bash

Classic

Download the app

Get the disk image and follow the five steps below. You only do the Privacy & Security part once; updates install without it.

Download for Mac

Requires macOS 14 Sonoma or later.

  1. STEP 1

    Drag QuitX into Applications

    Open the downloaded QuitX-vX.Y.Z.dmg and drag the QuitX icon onto the Applications folder.

  2. STEP 2

    Open QuitX, then click Done

    macOS says “QuitX” Not Opened because Apple hasn't checked it. Click Done. Don't choose Move to Trash.

  3. STEP 3

    Allow it in Privacy & Security

    Open System Settings › Privacy & Security and scroll to Security. Next to “QuitX” was blocked to protect your Mac, click Open Anyway. The button shows for about an hour after step 2.

  4. STEP 4

    Confirm with Open Anyway

    Click Open Anyway once more and enter your password or use Touch ID. macOS remembers your choice, so QuitX opens normally from now on.

  5. STEP 5

    Find it in your menu bar

    QuitX lives next to the clock. Click it to see every running app. Grant Accessibility later only if you want the window features.

On macOS 14 Sonoma

You can skip step 3: Control-click QuitX in Applications, choose Open, then click Open in the dialog.

Why the warning?

Notarization is Apple's malware scan for apps from paid developer accounts. QuitX skips it, but its source is public and every install or update is checked against GitHub's SHA-256 checksum and the app's code signature.

FAQ

Good questions.

Why does macOS say it can’t verify QuitX?

QuitX isn't notarized by Apple, because that needs a paid developer account. macOS asks you to confirm it once. Follow the install steps, or install from Terminal, which skips the prompt.

Will I lose unsaved work?

No. A normal quit asks each app to quit the same way the Dock does, so apps can still prompt you to save. QuitX only force quits when you choose it, for example by holding Option.

Which macOS versions are supported?

macOS 14 Sonoma and later. On macOS 26 you can switch between the classic look and Liquid Glass.

How do updates work?

QuitX checks GitHub Releases a few times a day when automatic checks are on. You'll see a red dot on the menu bar icon and an update item in the menu. The download is verified before QuitX restarts into the new version.

Why does it ask for Accessibility?

Only window-based features need it, such as quitting an app when its last window closes and the per-app Windows menu. You can use the rest of QuitX without granting it.

Is it really free?

Yes. QuitX is MIT licensed. There's no paid tier and no account.

Does QuitX collect or send my data?

No. QuitX has no account, analytics or tracking. It only connects to GitHub to check for and download updates.

Can I stop QuitX from closing specific apps?

Yes. Add any app to the Exclude list from its three-dot menu or Settings. You can also protect music apps with one switch.

What is the Ports feature?

It lists listening ports with their apps, processes and Docker containers, then lets you safely free selected ports. It is off by default; enable it in Settings → Ports.

Can QuitX uninstall apps and their leftovers?

Yes. QuitX finds the app, caches, preferences and support files for review, then moves selected items to the Trash. Apple and system apps are protected.

How can I support QuitX?

Star the project on GitHub, report useful bugs, or buy me a coffee. QuitX remains free and open source.
